Case study · Unsolicited redesign · Mobile UX

The 30-Minute Lie

A redesign of UCSD's dining app, built around one constraint: actually making it to 1pm lecture.

Role
Solo, research + design
Started from
Being late to lecture, repeatedly
Tools
Figma
Outcome
A pre-payment warning flow using class schedules from .ics files, with an escape hatch. Not yet user-tested (see reflection).

01 · The problem

The wait time is basically a guess

This started as me being annoyed, not as a design exercise. The app estimates pickup time the same way for every dish: active tickets times average prep time. But at the Bistro, a cold side and a hot wok entree are not the same. The wok station only has so many burners.

The app has no idea, so it shows a confident little timer that's wrong, and you end up standing outside in the wind way past when it said your food would be ready.

02 · Try it

Play with the redesign

Pick a dish, move the clock and the queue, then hit Place order. When the math says you'll be late, the app stops you before you pay, not after.

What you're ordering

Your next class: COGS 9 at 1:00 PM, about a 10-minute walk away. All numbers here are made up for the demo, not real Bistro data. The point is the shape of the logic: the wok station has a fixed number of burners, cold dishes don't wait for them.

03 · What the redesign changes

Friction on purpose, with an exit

Normally

Checkout should be as frictionless as possible.

Here

If the math says you'll be 3 minutes late to COGS 9, the app stops you right before payment. It felt wrong at first to add a deliberate speed bump, but that's the entire point.

Risk

The walking-time math assumes you're walking. Plenty of people are on a scooter or skateboard.

So

A plain "I'll walk fast, place order anyway" link. The app can warn you, but it shouldn't get to make the final call.

04 · A security call

Why .ics files instead of WebReg

My first instinct was to pull a student's schedule straight from WebReg. Then I realized that's a bad idea: it's UCSD's own login system, and scraping it is a real security problem. Almost every calendar app can export a standard .ics file, so I designed around that instead. Same result, far less risk, and the student never hands over a password to a class project.

05 · What I'd still test

Not finished until someone else uses it

I haven't gotten this in front of a student who isn't me yet, which is the obvious next step before I'd call this finished. I'm also not sure the modal should interrupt checkout every single time. There's a real chance it gets annoying if someone orders from the Bistro daily, and I'd want to watch someone hit "ignore" a few times before deciding if that's a problem.
