Case study · ACM AI Team 3 · Fall 2026

Plain Words, Same Facts

Hospital discharge notes are written for doctors. ClearMed rewrites them for patients, and checks every sentence so the rewrite never says something the original didn't.

Role
Readability agent owner + integration lead
Stack
Python, LangGraph, Ollama, textstat
Timeline
Oct 2026 – present
Outcome
In progress: pipeline wiring, then ablation tests and a demo.

01 · The problem

A wrong rewrite is worse than none

Only about 1 in 10 US adults can comfortably read everyday clinical information. People misread a dose or miss a follow-up, and some end up back in the hospital. AI can rewrite the text simply, but it can also make things up, like a dose or an instruction that was never there. In medicine, that's worse than no rewrite at all.

02 · The approach

Five agents instead of one

Instead of asking one model to do everything, our team splits the job. One agent pulls out the facts, one rewrites them in plain language, one checks every sentence against the original and flags anything unsupported, one fixes the flags, and the last one, mine, polishes the reading level. Everything runs on free, local open-source models, so anyone can reproduce it.

03 · What I found before building

The metric punished the explaining

Our target was an 8th-grade reading level. Before tuning my agent, I scored 20 pairs of expert-written plain-language rewrites on Flesch-Kincaid, SMOG and jargon density. None of the 20 reached grade 8.

In some pairs the "plain" version even scored harder, because experts keep the medical term and explain it in parentheses, and the formula punishes longer sentences. So the standard metric can penalize exactly the kind of explaining we want. That changed how I think about the agent's target: hitting a number isn't the goal if it means dropping the explanation a patient needs.

04 · What I'm building now

Wiring and measuring

I'm wiring the agents into a LangGraph pipeline with one bounded loop, where the checker and the fixer pass a draft back and forth at most twice, plus per-agent traces so we can see which agent changed what. Next up are ablation tests to measure which agents actually earn their place, and a demo.
